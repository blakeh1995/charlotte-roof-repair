Emergency Roof Repair: What to Do When Your Roof Fails

A branch through your roof. A hole from hail. Water pouring into your living room during a storm. Emergency roof damage doesn't wait, and neither should you.

First 30 Minutes: Stop the Water

Move furniture and valuables away from the leak. Put down buckets, towels, tarps—whatever stops water from spreading. Open windows for airflow (preventing mold growth). If there's standing water on the ceiling, use a small hole to drain it before the weight causes collapse.

If the damage is on the roof itself (branch sticking through), don't go up there alone. Wait for professionals. A wet roof is slippery, and an injury makes this worse.

First 2 Hours: Call a Roofer

Yes, it's evening or weekend. Real emergency contractors work 24/7 in Charlotte because hail and storms don't care about business hours. Call immediately. Describe the damage, your address, and whether water's entering your home. This determines priority.

A contractor with availability can often place a tarp over the damage same-day—that prevents another rain from making it worse. Cost: $200-500 for emergency tarp placement.

Document Everything

Take photos and videos from inside and outside. Show the water damage, the structural damage, everything. This matters for your insurance claim and your contractor's quote.

Write down the time the damage occurred, what caused it, and what you've done to prevent further damage. Insurance likes to see that you took action.

Call Insurance (If It's Storm-Related)

If a storm caused this, call your insurer immediately, even if it's late. Report the claim, get a claim number, and schedule the adjuster. The sooner they see it, the smoother the process.

Temporary vs. Permanent Repair

Emergency response is usually temporary: a tarp, a patch, something to keep water out until full repair or replacement. Temporary repairs buy you time to coordinate with insurance and get multiple quotes for the permanent fix.

Don't let a contractor tell you the tarp is enough long-term. It's not. It'll degrade in sun and fail in wind. Plan the permanent repair within 1-2 weeks.

Costs You'll Face

  • Emergency tarp (same-day): $200-500
  • Temporary patch: $300-800
  • Full repair/replacement: $800-15,000 depending on damage

If insurance covers it, you pay your deductible. If not, you're out of pocket, so get multiple quotes.
