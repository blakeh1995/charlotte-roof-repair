Ice Dams & Snow Melt Water Damage in Charlotte

Charlotte rarely gets heavy snow. When we do—combined with freeze-thaw cycles—water gets trapped on your roof. Here's what to watch for and how to prevent it.

How Ice Dams Form

Snow sits on your roof. Your attic is warm (from heating), so the snow melts from underneath. The meltwater runs down your roof but refreezes at the eaves (which are colder). Water backs up under your shingles. That's an ice dam, and water's now dripping into your walls.

Signs of Ice Dam Damage

  • Water stains on exterior walls below the roof line
  • Wet insulation in attic (especially after spring thaw)
  • Peeling paint inside near ceilings
  • Musty smell (mold from water sitting in walls)
  • Ice buildup on gutters and under eaves

Prevention

Good attic ventilation and insulation: Make sure your attic isn't too warm. The goal is to keep the roof surface cold so snow doesn't melt in the first place.

Clear gutters: Before winter, clean gutters completely. Water trapped in debris can freeze and block drainage.

Trim overhanging branches: Heavy snow loads on branches drop onto your roof, contributing to dam formation.

Check attic ventilation: Have a contractor verify your soffit and ridge vents aren't blocked. This lets warm air escape.

During Winter: What Not to Do

Don't climb on a snow-covered roof. Don't use salt or calcium chloride—they damage shingles and concrete. Don't bang ice dams to break them—you'll damage the roof.

If you have an active ice dam causing leaks, call a contractor. They have safe equipment to carefully remove ice without damaging the roof.

Repair Costs

If caught early (before water enters walls): $500-1,500 for attic inspection and drying.

If water's been sitting in walls for weeks: $1,500-5,000+ depending on mold remediation and structural drying needed.

Charlotte's humidity accelerates mold growth in wet walls. Don't wait.

Found Water Damage?

Get an inspection before spring. Mold and rotting wood develop quickly in Charlotte's humidity.

Long-Term Prevention

Good attic ventilation is your best defense. Have a contractor check it before winter. If your attic vents are blocked or undersized, that's an investment that pays off by preventing damage.
