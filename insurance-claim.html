Filing an Insurance Claim for Roof Damage: Timeline and Documentation

Your roof's damaged. Your insurance covers it (maybe). Now you navigate the claim process. Here's exactly how it works and what you need.

Step 1: Call Your Insurer (Within 24-30 Hours)

Have your policy number handy. Describe the damage, when it happened, and what caused it. Be factual: "Hail storm on May 25, 2026, caused dents in shingles and missing gutters." Insurance will assign a claim number and adjuster.

Step 2: Document Everything

Photos and video are your evidence. Take them fresh, same day or next day if possible. Insurance adjusters get skeptical about "damage" that could be normal wear.

Document:

  • Exterior damage (roof, gutters, siding, etc.) from ground level
  • Interior damage (water stains, wet insulation, damaged ceilings)
  • Close-ups of specific damage
  • Wide shots showing context (what part of the house, relation to other features)

Step 3: Get a Contractor Estimate

Insurance wants verification. Have a local roofer assess the damage and provide a detailed estimate. This gives insurance an independent opinion of cost and scope. If they low-ball you, the contractor's estimate is your evidence to dispute.

The Claim Timeline

Day 1: You call insurance, report claim, get claim number and adjuster contact
Day 2-5: Adjuster calls to schedule inspection (often can be same-day in urgent cases)
Day 5-10: Adjuster visits, takes photos, estimates damage
Day 10-14: Insurance reviews adjuster report, approves or denies claim
Day 14-21: If approved, insurer sends check to you minus deductible

The Deductible

This is YOUR cost before insurance pays. Standard deductibles are $500-2,000. If the insurer approves a $10,000 repair and your deductible is $1,000, you get a check for $9,000.

Some insurers offer "hurricane deductibles" (higher %) for wind and hail. Check your policy to know what applies.

What If They Deny?

Reasons they deny: The damage isn't storm-related (you say hail, they say wear and tear). Your roof's already damaged. The damage doesn't meet the policy threshold.

You can request a reconsideration, appeal to your state's insurance commissioner, or hire an independent adjuster to dispute. Independent adjusters cost $300-500 but can fight low settlements.

Pro Tips

  • Don't admit fault or cause uncertainty. "A storm caused this" is clear.
  • Keep all documentation: adjuster emails, photos, contractor estimates, claim correspondence.
  • Get everything in writing—verbal promises don't hold up.
  • If adjuster is unreasonable, ask for supervisor review or escalation.
