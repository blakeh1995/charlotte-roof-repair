Hail Damage Roof Repair: What Charlotte Homeowners Need to Know

Charlotte sits in a hail corridor. We get 14-20 hail events per year—more than most cities. If you've heard hail on your roof, you already know the sound: like gravel being thrown. What you can't see is the damage happening above your head.

How Hail Damages Roofs

Hail comes in different sizes. Pea-sized hail usually bounces off. Quarter-sized (and larger) hail dents asphalt shingles, cracks metal, and loosens the protective granules on asphalt. Each dent is a weak spot where the tar binding breaks. Water seeps in underneath.

Even if the shingle doesn't crack immediately, those dents are damage. They age faster than undamaged areas. Within a few years, those spots leak.

What Hail Damage Looks Like

Get in the attic or look at roof photos during the sun. Hail damage shows as round dents—not gouges or cracks. The dent is darker because the protective granules are gone, exposing the asphalt underneath.

On metal roofs, hail leaves dimples. They don't leak immediately (metal's tough), but they're still damage if your roof is insured.

Check Your Roof Right After Hail

Within 24 hours of a hail storm, get up there with a camera or binoculars. Document everything: dents, missing shingles, gutters full of granules. Insurance loves photos taken same-day. Don't wait a month—your adjuster can't tell if damage is from this storm or from normal aging.

File the Insurance Claim

Most homeowners insurance covers sudden hail damage. Call your insurer within 30 days of the storm—that's usually the deadline.

Here's the timeline:

  • Day 1: Call insurance company, report the claim, get a claim number
  • Day 2-5: Insurance assigns an adjuster, schedules inspection
  • Day 5-7: Adjuster visits, estimates damage
  • Day 10-14: Insurance approves or denies, sends you a check

If they approve, you're set. If they deny, you can dispute with a contractor's assessment or get a second opinion from an independent adjuster (you pay for that separately).

Getting a Contractor Quote

Don't wait for the insurance check to get quotes. Get 2-3 contractors to look while the damage is fresh. Their assessment helps with your claim if there's a dispute. Plus, you'll know the real cost before insurance decides.

Expect:

  • Roof repair (spot repairs for a few dents): $400-800
  • Roof replacement (widespread damage): $6,000-12,000 depending on size and material
  • Your insurance deductible: $500-2,000 (you pay this from the check)

What If Insurance Denies?

If your insurer says "that's not hail damage," ask for specifics. Get a second opinion from a local roofer. Some contractors offer independent damage assessments (usually $100-200) that insurance companies respect.

If you still disagree with the insurer, you can file an appeal. Many policies include mediation or appraisal clauses for disputed claims.

Prevention (You Can't, Really)

Hail happens. You can't prevent it. Keep your homeowners insurance current and your deductible reasonable. That's your real protection.
